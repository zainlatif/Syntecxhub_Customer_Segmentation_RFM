# Customer Segmentation Using RFM Analysis

## Executive Summary

This project applies Recency, Frequency, and Monetary (RFM) analysis to SuperStore sales transactions to identify customer groups that can be managed with differentiated retention, loyalty, and growth strategies.

The processed dataset contains **5,901 transaction rows**, **3,003 unique orders**, and **773 unique customers** covering **January 1, 2019 through December 31, 2020**. Total recorded sales are **$1,565,804.32**, with total profit of **$175,262.11**.

The analysis produces six actionable customer segments:

- Champions / Loyal Customers
- Potential Loyal Customers
- New Customers
- At Risk Customers
- Lost / Hibernating Customers
- Other Customers

The detailed calculations, tables, and charts are available in the [RFM analysis notebook](../notebooks/customer_segmentation_rfm.ipynb).

## Business Objective

The purpose of the analysis is to help the business:

- Recognize customers who generate high value and purchase consistently.
- Identify recently acquired customers who need encouragement to make a second purchase.
- Detect valuable customers whose activity has declined.
- Prioritize marketing resources by customer behavior rather than treating all customers alike.
- Support retention, cross-selling, referral, and reactivation decisions.

- Standardizes selected text fields before aggregation.

## Analytical Method

RFM metrics are calculated at customer level:

- **Recency:** Number of days since the customer's latest order.
- **Frequency:** Number of unique orders placed by the customer.
- **Monetary:** Total sales generated by the customer.

The review date is set to one day after the maximum order date in the cleaned dataset. This ensures the most recent purchase receives a recency value of one day rather than zero days and keeps the calculation reproducible.

Each RFM metric is scored from 1 to 5 using distribution-based quintiles. Recency is reverse-scored, so a more recent customer receives a higher score. Frequency and Monetary are scored in the normal direction, so customers with more orders or greater sales receive higher scores.

Frequency uses unique Order IDs rather than transaction-row counts. This avoids overstating customer activity when a single order contains multiple product lines.

## Segment Interpretation

| Segment | Behavioral profile | Recommended business action |
|---|---|---|
| Champions / Loyal Customers | High recency, frequency, and monetary scores | Offer VIP rewards, early access, referral benefits, and premium service. Protect this group from churn. |
| Potential Loyal Customers | Solid performance across the three RFM dimensions, but below champion level | Use personalized cross-sell offers, loyalty-program nudges, and purchase-frequency incentives. |
| New Customers | Recent activity with limited purchase frequency | Provide onboarding messages, a welcome offer, and a clear second-purchase incentive. |
| At Risk Customers | Previously active or valuable customers with weaker recency | Run targeted win-back campaigns, personalized outreach, and time-limited reactivation offers. |
| Lost / Hibernating Customers | Low recency and low frequency, generally with lower value | Use low-cost, selective re-engagement. Avoid spending heavily where expected return is low. |
| Other Customers | Mixed or intermediate RFM behavior | Apply category, region, and value-based targeting until a clearer behavior pattern emerges. |

The notebook calculates the customer count, percentage of customers, sales, profit, and average RFM metrics for every segment. These outputs should be treated as the operational source of truth when the data is refreshed.

## Key Findings

1. **Customer value is unevenly distributed.** A customer-level view is necessary because sales and order activity are concentrated across different behavioral profiles.
2. **Recency provides a direct retention signal.** Customers with older last-purchase dates should be addressed before their relationship becomes inactive.
3. **Frequency and Monetary distinguish loyalty from one-time value.** A customer with a large order but no repeat activity should receive a different treatment from a consistently high-value customer.
4. **Profit must accompany sales in decision-making.** High revenue does not automatically mean high profitability; the notebook retains and summarizes negative-profit transactions to expose this difference.
5. **Segment-specific actions are more efficient than broad campaigns.** The six groups provide a practical framework for allocating incentives, outreach effort, and customer-service attention.

## Recommended Actions and KPIs

### 1. Protect high-value customers

Prioritize Champions / Loyal Customers for VIP experiences, early product access, referral programs, and proactive service recovery.

Suggested KPIs: repeat-order rate, retention rate, average order value, and profit per customer.

### 2. Convert promising customers into loyal customers

Use cross-selling and loyalty prompts for Potential Loyal Customers. Recommendations should be informed by the categories and sub-categories purchased previously.

Suggested KPIs: second-order conversion, orders per customer, and category expansion rate.

### 3. Recover at-risk value

Contact At Risk Customers with personalized offers and reminders. Rank the campaign by prior monetary value and profit contribution so the highest-return opportunities are handled first.

Suggested KPIs: reactivation rate, recovered revenue, recovered profit, and campaign cost per reactivated customer.

### 4. Build early repeat behavior

Give New Customers a time-bound reason to return, supported by onboarding communication and relevant product recommendations.

Suggested KPIs: second-purchase rate within 30 or 60 days, time to second order, and welcome-offer redemption.

### 5. Test low-cost reactivation for dormant customers

Use a controlled test for Lost / Hibernating Customers. Suppress customers who do not respond after repeated attempts to prevent inefficient spend.

Suggested KPIs: incremental revenue, response rate, unsubscribe rate, and return on marketing spend.

## Limitations

- RFM describes historical transaction behavior and does not directly measure satisfaction, intent, or future demand.
- Quintile scores are relative to this dataset and may change when new transactions are added.
- Sales are used as the Monetary measure; a margin-adjusted or customer-lifetime-value measure may be more suitable for investment decisions.
- The source does not provide acquisition cost, campaign exposure, customer feedback, or a complete retention history.
- Segment labels are rule-based and should be reviewed against campaign results before being used for automated decision-making.
